1 — The Chemistry

MMA is a clear, volatile liquid monomer that polymerizes into PMMA (acrylic / Plexiglas). The danger here is not a reaction with water or air — it is runaway self-polymerization. MMA is shipped with a small amount of inhibitor (MEHQ, ~10–100 ppm) that prevents monomer molecules from linking. The inhibitor only works in the presence of dissolved oxygen, and it is consumed over time. Once depleted, polymerization begins, releases heat, accelerates, and in a sealed tank produces a self-reinforcing exotherm.

Why this one is hard to stop

  • Stuck off-load valve. Crews cannot pump product to a safe vessel or inject fresh inhibitor — the deluge is the only control lever.
  • O₂-starved inhibitor. A hot vapor-locked tank depletes dissolved O₂; MEHQ stops working even where it is present.
  • Trommsdorff gel effect. As MMA polymerizes and thickens, termination slows and the reaction self-accelerates — sharper than plain Arrhenius predicts.
  • Polymer-skin fouling. PMMA solidifies preferentially on cool surfaces — the inside of the tank wall where the deluge is hitting. PMMA's thermal conductivity is ~0.19 W/(m·K), almost 300× worse than steel. Even a 1 cm polymer skin doubles the wall's thermal resistance; 2 cm triples it. This is a brutal positive feedback: the chemistry that produces heat also produces the insulation that traps it.
  • Single observable. Only one risky interior reading per cycle (drone scans every 10 min are exterior proxies).

3 — The Model

A lumped-parameter energy balance — the standard structure used in thermal-runaway analysis — with three coupled ODEs:

k(T) = A · exp(−Ea / R·T) Arrhenius rate g(X) = 1 if X < 0.05 = 1 + 18·X^2.2 if X ≥ 0.05 Trommsdorff gel-effect amplifier dX/dt = k · (1−X) · (1−I) · g(X) conversion dI/dt = −c · k − c_O2·I·H(X − 0.005) MEHQ depletion + O₂-cliff (post-audit) δ(X) = f_plate · X · m / (ρ_PMMA · A_cool) polymer-skin layer thickness R_foul(X) = δ(X) / k_PMMA PMMA thermal resistance U_eff(X) = 1 / (1/U_clean + R_foul(X)) fouled heat-transfer coefficient Q_evap = h_m · A_cool · (P_sat(T_film) − RH·P_sat(T_air)) · MW_H₂O · h_fg / (R·T_film) deluge latent-heat cooling hod(t) = (t_hr + 15.667) mod 24 clock-aligned (Hour 0 = Thu 15:40 PDT) Q_solar(t) = S_max · max(0, cos(2π·(hod − 15)/24)) diurnal forcing dT/dt = (Q_gen − Q_cool) / (m · Cp) energy balance Q_gen = m · ΔH_p · dX/dt heat released by polymerization Q_cool = U_eff(X)·A · (T − T_water) + Q_evap − Q_solar fouled sensible + evaporative − solar

Three positive-feedback / negative-feedback terms govern the dynamics:

  • PMMA wall fouling (positive). k_PMMA = 0.19 W/(m·K) is roughly 300× more insulating than steel — at X = 2% effective cooling drops ~50%; at X = 10% it drops 85%. Chemistry that makes heat also makes the insulation that traps it.
  • O₂–MEHQ cliff (positive). MEHQ only inhibits in the presence of dissolved O₂. Once polymerization consumes the O₂ (X > ~0.005), MEHQ goes inert on minutes-to-hours, not the ~2,500 h that dI/dt = −c·k alone gave. This was missing pre-audit.
  • Evaporative cooling (negative — added latest). Water film on the hot shell evaporates at latent heat 2.26 MJ/kg. At T_bulk ≈ 90 °F, T_water = 75 °F, h_m = 0.02 m/s the evap term contributes ~26 kW vs ~18 kW sensible — roughly doubles the cooling capacity. Highly sensitive to h_m, which has order-of-magnitude operational uncertainty.

Explosion-risk criterion (Semenov tangency): heat generation grows exponentially with T while sensible cooling grows linearly. Once the gradient of Q_gen with T exceeds U_eff·A, no stable steady state exists. The evaporative term also grows nonlinearly with T (via P_sat), which is why adding it changes the steady-state structure, not just the magnitude.

Threshold calibration: the runaway line is set to the responders' own ~100 °F "out-of-control" estimate, not a textbook number. Their safe-neutralization target is ~50 °F. Both are verbal field figures, not lab values — see caveats.

5 — Monte Carlo Failure Window

10,000 accepted trajectories, drawn via Approximate Bayesian Computation: sample the uncertain parameters (Ea, U·A, I₀, f_plate, A_cool, T_water, h_m, RH, threshold T, PSV setpoint, T₀, solar amplitude) from broad priors, integrate the ODE forward from the measured 90 °F anchor at hour 30, and reject any trajectory that (a) crosses 100 °F or (b) would have cracked the PSV at any time between hour 12 (after the Thursday auto-vent settled) and hour 62 (Sun 6 am — last public survival anchor). The PSV setpoint is back-calibrated from the Thursday vent at ~95 °F bulk, giving ~1.5 psig ± 0.6; that constraint prunes an additional ~5% of trajectories that the runaway-threshold filter alone misses — slow-creep cases that would have visibly vented but not detonated. The remaining distribution is the posterior over future failure timing, conditioned on both pieces of survival evidence.

6 — Sensitivity Analysis

For each uncertain parameter, hold the others at the priors and pin this one to a low/high tail (~10th vs 90th percentile of its prior). Δ in crosses% tells you how much each input drives the answer. After adding evaporative cooling, the picture changed sharply: the deluge mass-transfer coefficient h_m is now the dominant lever (Δ ~16 pts), and ambient relative humidity is #2. Both of those control how much water actually evaporates per unit time off the wetted shell. The classic levers — UA, polymer fouling, even spray-water temperature — drop in importance because evap is the bigger cooling term once it's modeled.

Parameter sensitivity (tornado)

Bar length = absolute change in cross-rate (%) between low and high tails. Longer bar = more sensitive. Numbers near zero (I₀, Ea) mean the answer is robust to those inputs.

Operationally: the highest-leverage things crews can do are (1) maximize wetted-shell coverage and water flow (drives h_m up) and (2) operate when ambient air is drier (drives RH down, increases evaporation driving force). Both feed the latent-heat term. The model says a high-flow, well-distributed deluge in dry afternoon air is dramatically more effective than the same water in humid morning fog. Spray-water temperature, the previous top lever, drops to #3 once evap is in the model.

7 — Peak-Danger Hypothesis (first-pass, now superseded)

This section is preserved for the reasoning trail. The original hypothesis named Sunday 3–6 pm PDT as the dominant failure window from two converging arguments — but the calibrated 10k MC above has overruled that conclusion. Reading this section is useful for understanding how the analysis moved.

Thermodynamic (when could it tip?)

The most adverse heat balance of each day is the afternoon — warmest spray water, direct solar load on the steel shell, weakest deluge effectiveness. This argument still stands: failure mass within each day is afternoon-clustered. What it gets wrong is implying afternoons dominate across days. Once the survival filter and the evap-cooling term are in, the Sunday-afternoon window has only modest mass.

Kinetic (when does it get there?)

From 90 °F at Fri ~10 pm at ~1 °F/hr, the tank would reach the ~100 °F field threshold in 24–48 h — landing on Sunday afternoon. But that linear extrapolation ignores PMMA fouling, the deluge intensification, and evaporative cooling. The tank has actually held flat or below ~100 °F well past the linear-projection deadline. The MC's posterior median crossing time is now Sun late evening / Mon early morning, not Sun afternoon.

Covey's "slow cure" hypothesis — now confirmed by OCFA

Incident Commander Covey has now explicitly endorsed the slow-cure framing (LAist, Fri):

"One of the concepts we're thinking about is because of the heavy deluge of water and cooling it, we're allowing it to cure at a slower rate and reducing its overpressure building."

This is the operational thesis that the current model converged on independently: polymer-skin fouling means conversion is preferentially happening at the cold wall surface where heat is being extracted; evaporative cooling absorbs the released exotherm faster than it can build pressure. The model's high "holds" fraction (~95%) is consistent with the slow-cure end-state. OCFA has also confirmed that a controlled spill (not a detonation) is the "preferred outcome" — and has placed storm-drain containment to handle that branch.

What this section now is: a worked example of why first-pass synthesis from two clean arguments can be wrong when the system has positive-feedback dynamics — and how both the calibrated model and the on-scene incident commander converged on the same revised hypothesis (slow cure, eventual controlled spill) once enough new physics was in the model and enough survival evidence was in hand.

What the rings mean

  • 1 psi — window breakage, minor injuries from flying glass
  • 3 psi — serious injury, light building damage
  • 5 psi — wood-frame collapse, eardrum rupture
  • 10 psi — reinforced-building collapse, near-100% fatality
  • 4 kW/m² — pain in 20 s of exposure
  • 12.5 kW/m² — 1st-degree burns; ignition of wood after sustained exposure
  • 37.5 kW/m² — structural steel damage; 100% lethality in 1 min

Method & assumptions

  • Overpressure: Brode equation, free-air spherical TNT-equivalent
  • VCE yield 1–10% (3% is the common screening default for unconfined hydrocarbon clouds)
  • Thermal: point-source model with Xr=0.30 radiative fraction, MMA burn rate 0.04 kg/m²·s
  • Dispersion: ground-level continuous Gaussian (Pasquill-Gifford σy, σz) with a mild heavy-gas slump correction
  • Pool: 1-cm unbunded spread on flat ground
  • Cloud mass: 10% of released liquid assumed airborne & flammable (default screening fraction)
These are screening models, not engineering simulations. Real footprints depend on terrain, congestion (confined vs unconfined), specific ignition geometry, and atmospheric structure that simple Gaussian + TNT models do not capture. Use this to reason about scale — order-of-magnitude relationships between scenarios — not to plan response.

Caveats

  • The kinetic constants (A, Ea, ΔH) are representative MMA bulk values, not measured for this aged batch. A is phenomenologically tuned so the baseline reproduces the observed ~1 °F/hr early rise — calibration, not first-principles.
  • The 100 °F runaway threshold is a verbal field estimate from responders ("approximately 100 degrees" / "out of control") — not a lab figure for this tank. MC samples it as N(100, 4) °F.
  • The current posterior is dominated by the evaporative-cooling assumption. Adding latent-heat cooling shifted holds from 66% to ~94%. The mass-transfer coefficient h_m (sampled log-normal, median 0.012 m/s, ~10× range) is now the single most-sensitive parameter (Δ ≈ 16% over its range). If the actual deluge produces less evaporation than the prior median — e.g., because polymer-fouled steel doesn't wet well, or because the deluge isn't covering the full shell — the holds fraction collapses back toward 60–70%.
  • Other uncertain priors: polymer-plating fraction f_plate ~ N(0.35, 0.18) clipped [0.02, 0.75]; wetted area A_cool ~ N(40, 8) m² clipped [25, 70]; ambient RH ~ N(0.60, 0.10) clipped [0.30, 0.85]. Each captures a measurement we don't have.
  • Reported 60–61 °F Saturday readings are almost certainly exterior shell temperatures (deluge cools the steel skin while the interior keeps rising). Two sources (CBS LA, NBC LA) corroborate the number. Treated as a separate timeline series, not an interior anchor.
  • Drone scans every 10 minutes are exterior proxies; no new interior reading has been publicly released since Fri ~10 pm (90 °F).
  • The simulator is a lumped one-node model. A real stratified tank initiates runaway at the hot spot, not the bulk average — so the model is likely optimistic on timing (real tip-over could be earlier than the bulk model predicts).
  • The consequence model uses textbook screening formulas (Brode overpressure, point-source thermal radiation, Pasquill-Gifford dispersion). The audit flagged pool-fire burn rate (0.04 kg/m²/s) as ~2× literature for MMA (conservative), and the heavy-gas dispersion correction may underpredict near-field LEL distance (non-conservative).
  • Still-open audit findings (not yet acted on): Trommsdorff onset at X=0.05 in code vs literature X≈0.20; PSV venting dynamics absent (matters for spill-vs-rupture discrimination); hot-spot stratification absent (lumped 0-D); no Bayesian particle re-weighting on new readings; no historical-analog calibration.
