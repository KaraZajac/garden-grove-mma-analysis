1 — The Chemistry

MMA is a clear, volatile liquid monomer that polymerizes into PMMA (acrylic / Plexiglas). The danger here is not a reaction with water or air — it is runaway self-polymerization. MMA is shipped with a small amount of inhibitor (MEHQ, ~10–100 ppm) that prevents monomer molecules from linking. The inhibitor only works in the presence of dissolved oxygen, and it is consumed over time. Once depleted, polymerization begins, releases heat, accelerates, and in a sealed tank produces a self-reinforcing exotherm.

Why this one is hard to stop

  • Stuck off-load valve. Crews cannot pump product to a safe vessel or inject fresh inhibitor — the deluge is the only control lever.
  • O₂-starved inhibitor. A hot vapor-locked tank depletes dissolved O₂; MEHQ stops working even where it is present.
  • Trommsdorff gel effect. As MMA polymerizes and thickens, termination slows and the reaction self-accelerates — sharper than plain Arrhenius predicts.
  • Polymer-skin fouling. PMMA solidifies preferentially on cool surfaces — the inside of the tank wall where the deluge is hitting. PMMA's thermal conductivity is ~0.19 W/(m·K), almost 300× worse than steel. Even a 1 cm polymer skin doubles the wall's thermal resistance; 2 cm triples it. This is a brutal positive feedback: the chemistry that produces heat also produces the insulation that traps it.
  • Cracks observed Sun 5/24. A late-Sat-night recon mission discovered cracks in the tank shell. McGovern (Interim Chief): "potentially relieving some of the pressure." This is a partial passive vent — vapor can escape, dumping latent heat at ~360 kJ/kg. Helps. But cracks in a heating MMA vessel can also be the precursor to ductile-then-brittle failure (BLEVE precursor) — ambiguous.
  • Boiling-point threshold (Picazo, USC). The actual BLEVE physics threshold is MMA's boiling point ≈ 213 °F / 101 °C — above this, liquid→gas transition drives super-linear pressure rise that no PSV can keep up with. The earlier responder quote about "approximately 100 °F out of control" was a caution threshold, not a catastrophic one — confirmed empirically now that the gauge has pegged ≥100 °F without immediate failure.
  • Gauge ceiling. The interior gauge maxes at 100 °F. Once pegged (Sun 5:50 PM), the true bulk temperature is unknown — could be 105, could be 180. The model has to infer from the rate-of-rise history.

3 — The Model

A lumped-parameter energy balance — the standard structure used in thermal-runaway analysis — with three coupled ODEs:

k(T) = A · exp(−Ea / R·T) Arrhenius rate g(X) = 1 + 18·X^2.2 (for X ≥ 0.05; else 1) Trommsdorff gel-effect amplifier dX/dt = k · (1−X) · (1−I) · g(X) conversion dI/dt = −c · k − c_O2·I·H(X − 0.005) MEHQ + O₂-cliff δ(X) = f_plate · X · m / (ρ_PMMA · A_cool) polymer-skin thickness U_eff(X) = 1 / (1/U_clean + δ(X)/k_PMMA) fouled heat-transfer coefficient Q_evap = h_m · A_cool · (P_sat(T_film) − RH·P_sat(T_air)) · MW_H₂O · h_fg / (R·T_film) deluge latent-heat cooling Q_crack(T,t) = K_crack · max(0, T − (T_bp − 5)) for t ≥ 64 h crack-vent boiling cooling (NEW) P_total(T) = P_air₀·T/T₀ + P_MMA(T) vapor-space pressure (Antoine) hod(t) = (t_hr + 15.667) mod 24 clock-aligned local hour Q_solar(t) = S_max · max(0, cos(2π·(hod − 15)/24)) diurnal forcing dT/dt = (Q_gen − Q_cool) / (m · Cp) energy balance Q_gen = m · ΔH_p · dX/dt Q_cool = U_eff(X)·A · (T − T_water) + Q_evap + Q_crack − Q_solar Survival constraints (a trajectory is ACCEPTED only if ALL hold): (1) T < runawayF for all t ∈ [30, 75] BLEVE threshold not crossed (2) P_total < P_psv for all t ∈ [12, 64] no visible PSV vent (3) max T(t) ≥ 95 °F for t ≤ 74 gauge pegged ≥ 100 °F at hour 74

Feedback / loss terms governing the dynamics:

  • PMMA wall fouling (positive). At X = 2% effective cooling drops ~50%; at X = 10% it drops 85%.
  • O₂–MEHQ cliff (positive). Once polymerization consumes O₂ (X > ~0.005), inhibitor collapses on minutes-to-hours.
  • Evaporative cooling (negative). Water film latent heat ~26 kW at T_bulk ≈ 90 °F — roughly doubles sensible cooling. Highly sensitive to h_m (mass-transfer coefficient, order-of-magnitude operational uncertainty).
  • Crack-vent cooling (negative, post hour 64). MMA vapor escaping through the observed cracks dumps latent heat at ~360 kJ/kg. Ramps sharply as T approaches MMA's boiling point (213 °F); functionally caps trajectories near the boiling plateau if the rest of the system is balanced. Magnitude is approximate — no measurement of crack flow rate exists.

Explosion-risk criterion (Semenov tangency): heat generation grows exponentially with T while sensible cooling grows linearly. Once the gradient of Q_gen with T exceeds U_eff·A, no stable steady state exists. The evaporative term also grows nonlinearly with T (via P_sat), which is why adding it changes the steady-state structure, not just the magnitude.

Threshold calibration (REVISED Sun 5/24). The original 100 °F "out of control" responder quote was empirically falsified when the interior gauge pegged ≥100 °F at 5:50 PM PDT without immediate failure. Threshold prior is now centered at Picazo's BLEVE physics estimate: ~213 °F (MMA boiling at 1 atm), sampled N(210, 25) clipped to [160, 260]. The earlier 100 °F figure is reinterpreted as a CAUTION threshold — the line above which crews lose engineering margin — not the catastrophic-failure line.

6 — Sensitivity Analysis

For each uncertain parameter, hold the others at the priors and pin this one to a low/high tail. Δ in crosses% tells you how much each input drives the answer. After the paradigm shift, the picture became extreme: h_m (deluge mass-transfer coefficient) is overwhelmingly dominant at Δ ≈ 82%. Low h_m (0.004 m/s) puts crosses at ~99%; high h_m (0.035 m/s) drops it to ~17%. The deluge's effectiveness as an evaporative-cooling source is essentially the whole story now. Everything else — PSV setpoint, wetted area, polymer fouling, even threshold T — is secondary at single-digit Δ. If responders can make the deluge wet the shell more uniformly and at higher flow rate, that's the single intervention with leverage.

Parameter sensitivity (tornado)

Bar length = absolute change in cross-rate (%) between low and high tails. Longer bar = more sensitive. Numbers near zero (I₀, Ea) mean the answer is robust to those inputs.

Operational implication: the deluge is the entire game. The model now says a 9× swing in h_m moves the failure probability from ~17% to ~99%. Maximizing wetted-shell coverage (every square inch of steel wet at all times), maximizing water flow rate, and reducing humidity around the operation are the only levers with material impact at this point. Spray-water temperature itself dropped to #9 in the rankings — the temperature of the water matters far less than whether it's actually evaporating off the shell.

7 — Peak-Danger Hypothesis (first-pass, now superseded)

This section is preserved for the reasoning trail. The original hypothesis named Sunday 3–6 pm PDT as the dominant failure window from two converging arguments — but the calibrated 10k MC above has overruled that conclusion. Reading this section is useful for understanding how the analysis moved.

Thermodynamic (when could it tip?)

The most adverse heat balance of each day is the afternoon — warmest spray water, direct solar load on the steel shell, weakest deluge effectiveness. This argument still stands: failure mass within each day is afternoon-clustered. What it gets wrong is implying afternoons dominate across days. Once the survival filter and the evap-cooling term are in, the Sunday-afternoon window has only modest mass.

Kinetic (when does it get there?)

From 90 °F at Fri ~10 pm at ~1 °F/hr, the tank would reach the ~100 °F field threshold in 24–48 h — landing on Sunday afternoon. But that linear extrapolation ignores PMMA fouling, the deluge intensification, and evaporative cooling. The tank has actually held flat or below ~100 °F well past the linear-projection deadline. The MC's posterior median crossing time is now Sun late evening / Mon early morning, not Sun afternoon.

Two competing operational hypotheses, now in tension

Hypothesis A (OCFA / slow cure). Covey on Friday:

"Because of the heavy deluge of water and cooling it, we're allowing it to cure at a slower rate and reducing its overpressure building."

And McGovern on Sunday morning, after the crack discovery: "potentially relieving some of the pressure… a step in the right direction." OCFA's framing is that the system is in a controlled slow-cure end-state heading toward a manageable spill (the "preferred outcome"), with the cracks providing additional passive relief.

Hypothesis B (Picazo / BLEVE physics). USC chemistry professor Elias Picazo, the most-cited subject-matter expert across coverage, frames it more starkly:

"If you build too much heat or pressure, then you get the explosion. […] You can have fumes of toxins, flames like literal fireballs, and the explosion itself." Above MMA's boiling point (101 °C / 213 °F), "the pressure starts to increase dramatically, because the liquid phase becomes gas phase."

Picazo's threshold is a hard physical line. The interior gauge is now pegged at ≥100 °F (true T unknown — could be 105, could be 180). With the ~1 °F/hr historical rise rate and continued heating, the system is heading toward Picazo's BLEVE threshold; the only question is whether something stops it first (crack-vent absorbing the boiling latent heat? polymer block forming? a successful "outside the box" mitigation?).

Where the calibrated model lands

The 10k MC, conditioned on all three pieces of survival evidence (no BLEVE crossing, no PSV vent, T ≥ 95 °F by hour 74), produces a posterior that strongly favors Hypothesis B in the near term: 95% of accepted trajectories cross the BLEVE threshold within 24 hours of "now," with median crossing Sun ~10:41 PM PDT. The model does include crack-vent cooling (which caps T near 213 °F where it activates), but the threshold-prior is at the same temperature so the cap is barely above the threshold — trajectories tip just as the crack-vent term tries to take over.

The acceptance rate is 0.2%, which is itself important: most of the parameter space is INCONSISTENT with the observations. Either the model is missing physics (crack flow rate may be higher than assumed; PMMA fouling may saturate; stratification may keep bulk T below local hot-spot T) or the system really is in a narrow stable band on the edge of failure.

Honest read: the calibrated model says near-term BLEVE risk is high. OCFA's public optimism may reflect either (a) information we don't have (closer-look inspection of the cracks, specific mitigation strategies they haven't disclosed, etc.) or (b) the same hopeful framing every incident commander gives publicly until they can't anymore. The right posture for an outside observer is: take the model seriously, but keep watching the data — the next public temperature reading or visible-vent observation will break the tie between A and B fast.

What the rings mean

  • 1 psi — window breakage, minor injuries from flying glass
  • 3 psi — serious injury, light building damage
  • 5 psi — wood-frame collapse, eardrum rupture
  • 10 psi — reinforced-building collapse, near-100% fatality
  • 4 kW/m² — pain in 20 s of exposure
  • 12.5 kW/m² — 1st-degree burns; ignition of wood after sustained exposure
  • 37.5 kW/m² — structural steel damage; 100% lethality in 1 min

Method & assumptions

  • Overpressure: Brode equation, free-air spherical TNT-equivalent
  • VCE yield 1–10% (3% is the common screening default for unconfined hydrocarbon clouds)
  • Thermal: point-source model with Xr=0.30 radiative fraction, MMA burn rate 0.04 kg/m²·s
  • Dispersion: ground-level continuous Gaussian (Pasquill-Gifford σy, σz) with a mild heavy-gas slump correction
  • Pool: 1-cm unbunded spread on flat ground
  • Cloud mass: 10% of released liquid assumed airborne & flammable (default screening fraction)
These are screening models, not engineering simulations. Real footprints depend on terrain, congestion (confined vs unconfined), specific ignition geometry, and atmospheric structure that simple Gaussian + TNT models do not capture. Use this to reason about scale — order-of-magnitude relationships between scenarios — not to plan response.

12 — Caveats & limitations

Key recon findings

ItemValueNote

Caveats

  • 0.2% prior-acceptance is itself a warning. The MC rejects 99.8% of sampled parameter combinations because they violate one of the three survival constraints. That is Bayesian evidence the model is missing physics OR the system is genuinely in a very narrow stable band. Either interpretation deserves humility.
  • BLEVE threshold prior is centered on Picazo's BP-driven physics (~213 °F) with N(210, 25) °F. This is the most defensible single anchor in the published expert commentary, but every accepted trajectory in the current MC has a threshold below 213 °F and crosses it. If the actual threshold is materially above 213 °F (because PSV/crack venting holds pressure low), the model is too pessimistic.
  • Crack-vent cooling term is NEW and approximate. No measurement of crack flow rate exists; the model uses a stiff negative-feedback term scaled to give ~50 kW at T = BP. Tuning K_crack higher would soften the failure prediction substantially. This is the single largest open uncertainty in the current model.
  • Kinetic constants (A, Ea, ΔH) are representative MMA bulk values, not measured for this aged batch. A is phenomenologically tuned so the baseline reproduces the observed ~1 °F/hr early rise — calibration, not first-principles.
  • The interior gauge maxes at 100 °F. Since pegging Sun 5:50 PM PDT, true bulk temperature is unknown — could be 105, could be 180. Model has to infer from rate-of-rise history.
  • Reported 60–61 °F Saturday readings are almost certainly exterior shell temperatures (deluge cools the steel skin while the interior keeps rising). Two sources (CBS LA, NBC LA) corroborate the number. Treated as a separate timeline series, not an interior anchor.
  • Drone scans every 10 minutes are exterior proxies; no new interior reading has been publicly released since Fri ~10 pm (90 °F).
  • The simulator is a lumped one-node model. A real stratified tank initiates runaway at the hot spot, not the bulk average — so the model is likely optimistic on timing (real tip-over could be earlier than the bulk model predicts).
  • The consequence model uses textbook screening formulas (Brode overpressure, point-source thermal radiation, Pasquill-Gifford dispersion). The audit flagged pool-fire burn rate (0.04 kg/m²/s) as ~2× literature for MMA (conservative), and the heavy-gas dispersion correction may underpredict near-field LEL distance (non-conservative).
  • Still-open audit findings (not yet acted on): Trommsdorff onset at X=0.05 in code vs literature X≈0.20; PSV venting dynamics absent (matters for spill-vs-rupture discrimination); hot-spot stratification absent (lumped 0-D); no Bayesian particle re-weighting on new readings; no historical-analog calibration.
