1 — The Chemistry

MMA is a clear, volatile liquid monomer that polymerizes into PMMA (acrylic / Plexiglas). The danger here is not a reaction with water or air — it is runaway self-polymerization. MMA is shipped with a small amount of inhibitor (MEHQ, ~10–100 ppm) that prevents monomer molecules from linking. The inhibitor only works in the presence of dissolved oxygen, and it is consumed over time. Once depleted, polymerization begins, releases heat, accelerates, and in a sealed tank produces a self-reinforcing exotherm.

Why this one is hard to stop

  • Stuck off-load valve. Crews cannot pump product to a safe vessel or inject fresh inhibitor — the deluge is the only control lever.
  • O₂-starved inhibitor. A hot vapor-locked tank depletes dissolved O₂; MEHQ stops working even where it is present.
  • Trommsdorff gel effect. As MMA polymerizes and thickens, termination slows and the reaction self-accelerates — sharper than plain Arrhenius predicts.
  • Single observable. Only one risky interior reading per cycle (drone scans every 10 min are exterior proxies).

3 — The Model

A lumped-parameter energy balance — the standard structure used in thermal-runaway analysis — with three coupled ODEs:

k(T) = A · exp(−Ea / R·T) Arrhenius rate dX/dt = k · (1−X) · (1−I) · g(X) conversion (g = Trommsdorff gel-effect amplifier) dI/dt = −c · k inhibitor (MEHQ) depletion dT/dt = (Q_gen − Q_cool) / (m · Cp) energy balance Q_gen = m · ΔH_p · dX/dt heat released by polymerization Q_cool = U·A · (T − T_water) − Q_solar external cooling minus solar gain

Explosion-risk criterion (Semenov tangency): heat generation grows exponentially with temperature while cooling only grows linearly. Once the gradient of Q_gen with T exceeds U·A, no stable steady state exists and the temperature climbs toward the adiabatic rise that MMA's heat of polymerization dictates.

Threshold calibration: the runaway line is set to the responders' own ~100 °F "out-of-control" estimate, not a textbook number. Their safe-neutralization target is ~50 °F. Both are verbal field figures, not lab values — see caveats.

6 — Peak-Danger Hypothesis

Two independent derivations converge on the same window, which is what elevates this from guess to hypothesis:

Thermodynamic (when can it tip?)

The most adverse heat balance of each day is the afternoon — warmest spray water (~75 °F), direct solar load on the steel shell, weakest deluge effectiveness. If a runaway initiates, it initiates in the afternoon, not overnight.

Kinetic (when does it get there?)

From 90 °F at Fri ~10 pm at ~1 °F/hr, the tank reaches the ~100 °F field threshold in the 24–48 h window — landing squarely on Sunday afternoon into Sunday night.

Competing hypothesis (slow cure)

Incident Commander Covey has floated that the heavy deluge may be letting the tank "cure at a slower rate," spreading the polymerization energy out over time. If correct, there is no single runaway moment — the reactive monomer bleeds off gradually and the failure mode is a spill, not a detonation. The fact that the tank survived Saturday afternoon's solar peak is weak but real evidence in this direction.

Confidence: MODERATE on the timing class (afternoon). LOW–MODERATE on the specific day. NOT ASSESSED for absolute probability of failure — that is a hypothesis, not a prediction.

What the rings mean

  • 1 psi — window breakage, minor injuries from flying glass
  • 3 psi — serious injury, light building damage
  • 5 psi — wood-frame collapse, eardrum rupture
  • 10 psi — reinforced-building collapse, near-100% fatality
  • 4 kW/m² — pain in 20 s of exposure
  • 12.5 kW/m² — 1st-degree burns; ignition of wood after sustained exposure
  • 37.5 kW/m² — structural steel damage; 100% lethality in 1 min

Method & assumptions

  • Overpressure: Brode equation, free-air spherical TNT-equivalent
  • VCE yield 1–10% (3% is the common screening default for unconfined hydrocarbon clouds)
  • Thermal: point-source model with Xr=0.30 radiative fraction, MMA burn rate 0.04 kg/m²·s
  • Dispersion: ground-level continuous Gaussian (Pasquill-Gifford σy, σz) with a mild heavy-gas slump correction
  • Pool: 1-cm unbunded spread on flat ground
  • Cloud mass: 10% of released liquid assumed airborne & flammable (default screening fraction)
These are screening models, not engineering simulations. Real footprints depend on terrain, congestion (confined vs unconfined), specific ignition geometry, and atmospheric structure that simple Gaussian + TNT models do not capture. Use this to reason about scale — order-of-magnitude relationships between scenarios — not to plan response.

10 — Caveats & limitations

Key recon findings

ItemValueNote

Caveats

  • The kinetic constants (A, Ea, ΔH) are representative MMA bulk values, not measured for this exact aged, partially-polymerized batch. The pre-exponential A is phenomenologically tuned so the baseline trajectory reproduces the observed ~1 °F/hr early rise — that is a calibration, not a first-principles result.
  • The 100 °F runaway threshold is a verbal field estimate from responders ("approximately 100 degrees" / "out of control") — not a lab figure for this tank. The MC samples it as N(100, 4) °F.
  • The 78/22 split is the posterior given survival to hour 54. It is sensitive to the priors (especially the cooling-effectiveness log-normal). What is robust is the shape — afternoons load-bearing, nights nearly empty.
  • Reported 60–61 °F Saturday readings are almost certainly exterior shell temperatures (deluge cools the steel skin while the interior keeps rising). Two sources (CBS LA, NBC LA) corroborate the number; chats with the field response history previously flagged the same magnitude as misleading. Treated as a separate timeline series, not an interior anchor.
  • Drone scans every 10 minutes are exterior proxies; no new interior reading has been publicly released since Fri ~10 pm (90 °F).
  • The simulator is a lumped one-node model. A real stratified tank initiates runaway at the hot spot, not the bulk average — so the model is likely conservative on timing.
  • The consequence model uses textbook screening formulas (Brode overpressure, point-source thermal radiation, Pasquill-Gifford dispersion with mild heavy-gas correction). Real footprints depend on terrain and congestion that these formulas do not capture.
