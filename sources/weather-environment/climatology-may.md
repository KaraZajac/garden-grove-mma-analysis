## Annual Climate Context (Santa Ana / KSNA)

- Annual mean high ~76°F; annual mean low ~56°F
- Annual precipitation ~12.5 inches (Mediterranean / semi-arid)
- Annual rainy days ~28
- Climate type: Köppen **Csa** (hot-summer Mediterranean), maritime-influenced
- Prevailing wind pattern late spring: **onshore SW sea-breeze**; mornings calm with low overcast ("May Gray"), afternoons partly clearing with peak SW winds of 8-15 mph and occasional gusts to ~20 mph
- Santa Ana (offshore NE/E) wind events are rare in May (climatologically <1 day per May)
- Marine inversion is typical at 1,500-2,500 ft, capping mixing layer and limiting vertical dispersion

## Implications for Incident

The current forecast (see `nws-forecast-garden-grove.md` and `wind-forecast.md`) is **essentially typical late-May weather** for Garden Grove - cool, overcast mornings transitioning to mild partly sunny afternoons with light to moderate SW winds. No anomalous heat, no Santa Ana wind, no precipitation. This means:
- Tank surface heating from solar load is **moderate**, not extreme (peak daytime air ~72°F, peak insolation reduced by marine clouds on Sun/Mon)
- Vapor transport is **consistent and predictable** (SW → NE)
- No precipitation to wash out vapors or runoff containment concerns
- No fog event likely; visibility remains good for aerial monitoring
- Conditions are favorable for tactical operations: cooler-than-summer ambient temperature reduces evaporation rate; consistent SW wind gives planners a stable downwind sector for evacuation prioritization
