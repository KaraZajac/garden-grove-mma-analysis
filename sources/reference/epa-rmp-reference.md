# EPA Risk Management Program — Reference for an MMA Storage Tank

## What is the RMP rule?

The Risk Management Program rule, promulgated under Section 112(r) of the Clean Air Act and codified at **40 CFR Part 68**, requires facilities that handle more than a **threshold quantity** of a **listed regulated substance** in a **process** to develop and submit a **Risk Management Plan** to EPA.

## List of Regulated Substances — Structure

The §112(r) list (40 CFR 68.130) contains both **toxic** and **flammable** substances, each with an assigned **threshold quantity (TQ)** in pounds. If the maximum quantity held in a process meets or exceeds the TQ, the facility is in scope.

### Threshold Quantity Tiers (examples)

| TQ (lbs) | Example substances |
|----------|--------------------|
| 1 000 | arsine, fluorine, hydrogen fluoride |
| 2 500 | diborane, titanium tetrachloride |
| 5 000 | acrolein, phosphine, sulfur dioxide |
| 10 000 | majority of listed flammables (alkenes, aldehydes, flammable gases) |
| 15 000 | allyl alcohol, hydrazine, formaldehyde |
| 20 000 | acrylonitrile, chloroform, epichlorohydrin |

## Is Methyl Methacrylate on the List?

**Per the current 40 CFR 68.130 list of regulated substances, methyl methacrylate (CAS 80-62-6) is NOT listed** — neither as a toxic nor as a flammable. It therefore does not, by itself, trigger an RMP filing.

However, several considerations remain:

1. **Other chemicals on site** — if the same facility holds RMP-listed flammables (propylene, butane, acetaldehyde, etc.) or toxics above their TQ, the facility files an RMP for those processes. An MMA tank could fall under the same "Program 3" requirements if it is part of a regulated process.

2. **Clean Air Act §112(r)(1) General Duty Clause** — applies even to facilities not subject to the RMP rule:
   > "Owners and operators of stationary sources... have a general duty... to identify hazards, design and maintain a safe facility, and minimize the consequences of accidental releases of any extremely hazardous substance."
   MMA, as a flammable monomer prone to runaway polymerization, clearly fits "extremely hazardous substance" for general-duty purposes.

3. **OSHA Process Safety Management (29 CFR 1910.119)** — covers processes with > 10 000 lb of flammable liquids regardless of the EPA list, with limited exclusions. Most large MMA storage installations are covered.

4. **CAA HAP listing** — methyl methacrylate is a listed Hazardous Air Pollutant (HAP) under Section 112(b). Emission standards (NESHAP) apply to facilities emitting above thresholds.

## RMP Program Levels (40 CFR 68.10)

- **Program 1** — minimal off-site consequences; simple registration + 5-year accident history
- **Program 2** — streamlined prevention program; small/simple processes
- **Program 3** — full prevention program; applies to PSM-covered processes and certain SIC codes (industrial chemical manufacturing, petroleum refining, pulp mills, etc.)

A large bulk MMA storage tank at a chemicals manufacturer would, if RMP-regulated through other listed substances on site, generally fall under **Program 3**.

## Required RMP Contents (40 CFR Part 68 Subpart G — §68.150–68.190)

A submitted Risk Management Plan must contain:

1. **Executive Summary** — facility description, accidental release prevention/emergency response policies, hazards, prevention program summary, five-year accident history
2. **Registration** (§68.160) — name, address, parent company, NAICS codes, EPA Facility ID, list of regulated substances, distance to nearest public receptor
3. **Off-site Consequence Analysis (OCA)** (§68.165) — for the worst-case and alternative release scenarios:
   - quantity released, release rate, duration
   - endpoint(s) used (toxic endpoint or 1 psi overpressure for flammables)
   - distance to endpoint
   - population within distance
   - environmental receptors
4. **Five-year accident history** (§68.168) — for releases above thresholds: date, time, duration, release type, quantity, weather, on-site/off-site effects, initiating event, contributing factors, change introduced post-incident
5. **Prevention program** (§68.170–68.175) — for each Program 2/3 process:
   - process safety information
   - hazard review / PHA
   - operating procedures
   - training
   - maintenance
   - compliance audits
   - incident investigation
   - management of change (Program 3)
   - pre-startup review (Program 3)
   - mechanical integrity (Program 3)
   - employee participation (Program 3)
   - hot work permits (Program 3)
   - contractor program (Program 3)
6. **Emergency response program** (§68.180) — if responding employees handle releases:
   - emergency response plan
   - first-aid and medical procedures
   - emergency equipment
   - training
   - coordination with local responders

## What an MMA Tank Filing Would Look Like (if covered)

For a bulk MMA storage tank, the RMP would document:

- **Process safety info:** chemistry (MMA polymerization), heat of polymerization (~57 kJ/mol), inhibitor regime (MEHQ ppm, dissolved O₂ requirement), thermal stability data, materials of construction
- **Hazard review:** scenarios of inhibitor depletion, loss of cooling, contamination, fire exposure, runaway polymerization, BLEVE
- **Worst-case scenario:** rupture or 10-min release of the entire tank contents as flammable vapor cloud; 1 psi overpressure distance via TNT-equivalent or vapor-cloud-explosion model
- **Alternative scenarios:** pump seal failure, tank fitting leak, etc.
- **Five-year history:** any releases ≥ TQ-fraction
- **Prevention:** temperature/pressure monitoring with high-T alarms and trips; inhibitor concentration testing schedule; O₂ monitoring of headspace; nitrogen/air blend system; agitation if any; relief valves sized for runaway polymerization (note ANSI/AIChE DIERS methodology)
- **Emergency response:** evacuation plan; coordination with local fire and LEPC

## RMP Update Frequency (§68.190)

- Full update at least every **5 years**
- Within **6 months** of any change to off-site consequence analysis, process, hazards, prevention program, or emergency response program
- Within **6 months** of a change in regulated substance or quantity above threshold
- Within **1 month** of an emergency response program change

## Public Access

RMPs are submitted electronically through CDX/RMP*eSubmit. Most plan content is publicly available; the off-site consequence analysis (OCA) portion is restricted under Chemical Safety Information, Site Security and Fuels Regulatory Relief Act (CSISSFRRA) and accessible only in reading rooms.
