# Poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA)

**Source:** Wikipedia · **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poly(methyl_methacrylate)

## Introduction

Poly(methyl methacrylate), PMMA, is a synthetic transparent thermoplastic polymer derived from methyl methacrylate. Trade names include Plexiglas, Perspex, Lucite.

## Historical Development

- 1843 — acrylic acid synthesized
- 1865 — methacrylic acid synthesized
- 1928 — polymerization of MMA reported by several laboratories (Conn, Röhm, Bauer)
- 1933 — Röhm & Haas AG markets Plexiglas in Germany; ICI registers Perspex in UK
- WWII — both Allied and Axis militaries used acrylic glass in submarine periscopes and aircraft canopies

## Chemical Properties and Structure

- IUPAC name: poly(methyl 2-methylpropenoate)
- Chemical formula: (C₅H₈O₂)ₙ

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Density | 1.17 – 1.20 g/cm³ |
| Refractive index | 1.4905 at 589.3 nm |
| Glass transition temperature (Tg, atactic) | 105 °C |
| Commercial Tg range | 85 – 165 °C |
| Light transmission | up to 92 % visible (3 mm) |
| Ignition temperature | 460 °C |
| Reflection | ~4 % |
| Max water absorption | 0.3 – 0.4 % wt |

UV filtration: blocks wavelengths < ~300 nm (similar to window glass).

## Properties

- Roughly half the density of glass, strong optical clarity
- Higher impact strength than glass and polystyrene; significantly lower than polycarbonate
- Unmodified PMMA brittle; modified grades achieve high scratch/impact resistance
- Water absorption reduces tensile strength

## Synthesis and Production

Three approaches: **emulsion**, **solution**, and **bulk** polymerization. Radical initiation is dominant industrially; anionic polymerization is feasible.

**Cast acrylic** — MMA monomer + initiators cast into static or rotational molds; superior thermal stability and optical properties vs extruded.

## Processing

- Injection molding, compression molding, extrusion
- Joined with cyanoacrylate cement, thermal welding, or chlorinated solvents (e.g. dichloromethane, which dissolves PMMA at the joint)
- Laser cutting for intricate designs (vaporizes polymer); pulsed laser cuts cause stress-crazing on later solvent exposure

## Recycling

1. **Pyrolysis** above 400 °C — recovers monomer; uneconomic
2. **Chain-end depolymerization** — up to 90 % monomer yield (less with dyes)
3. **Most promising:** chlorinated solvent + crushed material + 90–150 °C + UV — yields **94 – 98 %** virgin-grade material

## Medical and Dental Applications

- Discovered to be biocompatible by Harold Ridley examining RAF pilots with PMMA aircraft-window fragments in their eyes
- First intraocular lens implanted at St Thomas' Hospital, London, **29 November 1949**
- Dentures, denture bases, orthodontic appliances
- Orthopedic bone cement (with MMA monomer — irritant and possible carcinogen)

## Industrial / Consumer Applications

- Aquariums (transparent sheet under water pressure)
- Aviation windows
- Automotive exterior light lenses
- Architecture (Munich 1972 Olympic Park roof)
- Illuminated signage
- TFT-LCD light guides
- Furniture, sculpture, fine art

## Comparison to Polycarbonate

PMMA preferred when tensile strength, flexural strength, transparency, polishability, and UV tolerance matter more than impact strength. PMMA does **not** contain BPA. More stable than polystyrene/polyethylene outdoors; susceptible to organic solvents due to easily hydrolyzed ester groups.
