## Section 1 — Identification

- **Product:** Methyl methacrylate (monomer)
- **Synonyms:** MMA; 2-(methoxycarbonyl)-1-propene; methyl 2-methylpropenoate; methyl 2-methyl-2-propenoate
- **CAS:** 80-62-6
- **EC:** 201-297-1
- **Formula:** C₅H₈O₂
- **MW:** 100.12 g/mol
- **DOT/UN:** UN 1247, Methyl methacrylate monomer, stabilized; Class 3 flammable liquid, PG II

## Section 2 — Hazards

- **GHS pictograms:** GHS02 (flame), GHS07 (exclamation)
- **Signal word:** Danger
- **Hazard statements:** H225 (highly flammable liquid and vapor), H315 (skin irritation), H317 (sensitization), H335 (respiratory irritation)
- **Other:** Vapors uninhibited and may polymerize, causing blockage of vents. If polymerization takes place in a container, the container is subject to violent rupture.

## Section 3 — Composition

- **MMA:** ≥ 99 % (typical commercial spec)
- **Inhibitor (typical):** **MEHQ (hydroquinone monomethyl ether), 10–100 ppm** (most common commercial spec ~25 ppm; some grades include hydroquinone or 2,6-di-tert-butyl-4-methylphenol at low ppm)
- Sigma-Aldrich technical literature notes commercial MMA may contain hydroquinone, hydroquinone methyl ether, and dimethyl-t-butylphenol as inhibitors

## Section 4 — First Aid

- **Inhalation:** Move to fresh air; medical attention if symptoms persist
- **Skin:** Wash with soap and water; remove contaminated clothing
- **Eyes:** Flush with water 15+ minutes
- **Ingestion:** Do not induce vomiting; seek medical attention

## Section 5 — Firefighting

- Suitable: dry chemical, CO₂, alcohol-resistant foam, water spray (large fires)
- Vapor heavier than air; may travel to ignition sources and flash back
- Fire-exposed containers may rupture from polymerization pressure
- **Cool containers with water spray; protect against vapor cloud / BLEVE-type behavior**
- PPE: full bunker gear + SCBA

## Section 6 — Accidental Release

- Eliminate ignition sources
- Ventilate
- Absorb with inert material (vermiculite, sand)
- Prevent entry to waterways / sewers
- Vapor suppression with water spray or foam
- All electrical equipment must be intrinsically safe (vapor in flammable range = 1.7 – 8.2 % vol)

## Section 7 — Handling & Storage

**Handling**
- Avoid heat, sparks, open flames, hot surfaces (flash point 2 °C)
- Bonding/grounding of transfer equipment
- Avoid contact with oxidizers, peroxides, strong acids/bases, polymerization initiators
- Avoid extended contact with copper, copper alloys, lead — can initiate polymerization
- Compatible: stainless steel, aluminum, galvanized steel
- **Incompatible: cast/ductile iron, carbon steel** (per SafeRack bulk-handling guidance)

**Storage**
- Cool, dry, well-ventilated
- **Below 25 °C** preferred; typical industrial guidance is **below 10 °C** for long-term storage
- **DO NOT inert with pure nitrogen** — MEHQ requires dissolved O₂ to function. Use air or 50/50 N₂/air headspace.
- Keep away from light, heat, ionizing radiation
- Monitor inhibitor concentration and temperature in long-term storage
- DOT-115 insulated tank cars for rail; DOT-approved tank trailers (MC 300-312, MC 330-331)

## Section 8 — Exposure Controls / PPE

- **OSHA PEL:** 100 ppm TWA (410 mg/m³)
- **NIOSH REL:** 100 ppm TWA (410 mg/m³)
- **NIOSH IDLH:** 1 000 ppm
- **ACGIH TLV-TWA:** 50 ppm; STEL 100 ppm (typical industry adoption)
- Engineering: local exhaust, explosion-proof ventilation
- PPE: chemical-splash goggles; chemical-resistant gloves (butyl, Viton); chemical-resistant apron; respirator if engineering controls inadequate

## Section 10 — Stability & Reactivity

- **Reactive Hazard:** YES
- **Hazardous polymerization:** **WILL OCCUR** in the absence of inhibitor, on heating, exposure to UV/sunlight, ionizing radiation, peroxides, or strong acids
- **Conditions to avoid:** heat, sparks, open flame, sunlight, contact with metals (Cu, Pb, Fe-based), loss of inhibitor, loss of dissolved O₂ in stored monomer
- **Incompatibilities:** strong oxidizers, peroxides, free-radical initiators (AIBN, BPO), strong acids/bases, azo compounds, certain metal salts
- **Decomposition products:** CO, CO₂; if polymerization is uncontrolled, runaway exotherm → boiling MMA vapor → potential vapor cloud explosion
- Polymerization is highly exothermic and **auto-accelerating** (Trommsdorff-Norrish gel effect; see `trommsdorff-effect.md`)

## Section 11 — Toxicology

- **Acute oral LD₅₀ (rat):** 8 420 – 10 000 mg/kg
- **Inhalation LC₅₀ (rat, 4 h):** 18 750 ppm
- **Skin:** mild irritant; sensitizer in susceptible individuals
- **Eyes:** moderate irritant
- **Respiratory:** nasal/throat irritation at higher levels
- **Carcinogenicity:** IARC Group 3 (not classifiable); not listed by NTP; possible carcinogen designation in some jurisdictions
- **Reproductive/developmental:** not classified as reproductive toxin under most schemes

## Section 12 — Ecology

- Aquatic toxicity: moderate (96-h LC₅₀ fish ~100–300 mg/L)
- Biodegradable; not bioaccumulative (log Pow 1.35)
- Volatile — partitions to air

## Section 13 — Disposal

Burn in approved high-temperature incinerator. Comply with all federal, state, and local regulations. RCRA waste code: D001 (ignitable).

## Section 14 — Transport

- **UN 1247** Methyl methacrylate monomer, stabilized
- Class 3 (flammable liquid)
- Packing Group II
- DOT-115 / DOT-105J tank cars for rail
- IATA: Class 3, PG II — limited quantities apply

## Section 15 — Regulatory

- **U.S. Clean Air Act:** listed Hazardous Air Pollutant (HAP)
- **TSCA:** listed
- **SARA Title III §313:** reportable
- **EPA RMP (40 CFR 68):** **MMA is not on the §112(r) list of regulated substances** (not assigned a threshold quantity under RMP per the current list). However, an MMA facility may still be subject to **OSHA PSM (29 CFR 1910.119)** if MMA is processed with other listed chemicals; and to general-duty requirements under §112(r)(1) of the CAA.
- **California Prop 65:** not listed (as of last reference check)
