# Rohm & Haas 2nd MMA Tank Car Release — Louisville, KY (June 2010)

**Source:** EPA On-Scene Coordinator Response site profile
**URL:** https://response.epa.gov/site/site_profile.aspx?site_id=6137

> Note: this incident does NOT appear to have produced a dedicated CSB investigation report. The primary public record is the EPA OSC site profile. A similar release at the same facility in 2008 is referenced for context.

## Location
4300 Campground Road, Louisville, KY 40216. Facility operated by Dow Chemical Company (former Rohm and Haas plant).

## Material
Methyl methacrylate (MMA), specifically a stream identified as **"3rd vent monomer"** — a process side-stream MMA.

## Quantities
- Tank car load: **175 000 lbs** of MMA product
- Released to atmosphere: **17 500 lbs** (~10 %)

## Equipment
**DOT 105-J tank car** — specifically designed for monomer service, built to withstand internal pressures from polymerization without rupturing.

## Timeline (all dates 2010 unless noted)

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| Jun 13, 1230 hr | Tank car discovered venting MMA; pressure build-up indicating polymerization had begun |
| Jun 13, ~1600 hr | Shelter-in-place lifted |
| Jun 13, 1700 hr | EPA On-Scene Coordinator deployed |
| Jun 13, 2130 hr | OSC demobilized |
| Jun 14, 0200 hr | Release ended |
| Jun 14, 0900 hr | Lake Dreamland Fire Department relinquished incident control |

Total release lasted ~13.5 hours.

## What Occurred

Tank car experienced internal pressure buildup. The 105-J pressure-relief design vented the over-pressurization rather than rupturing — by design behavior — releasing MMA vapor. Pressure rise was attributed to **polymerization initiating inside the tank car.**

Reported per Kentucky SERC dated 2010-06-23: 17 500 lbs released total.

## Response Actions

- Shelter-in-place issued as precaution for adjacent off-site areas; lifted same afternoon
- Perimeter air monitoring along site — **non-detect for VOCs**
- Vapor-suppression water spray applied to tank car
- Runoff sampled — **non-detect for residual monomer** at 1 mg/L quantitation limit
- Lake Dreamland Fire Department commanded scene with continuous air monitoring

## Historical Context — 2008 Predecessor Incident

EPA responded to a similar incident at the same facility in 2008. In that earlier event the MMA inside the tank car **"hardened up like plexiglass after about 8 days"** — i.e. completed polymerization in-situ before the car could be emptied.

That a near-identical incident recurred in 2010 indicates the facility had not implemented effective countermeasures for monomer-stability monitoring or aged-inventory management in the two years between incidents.

## Lessons Relevant to MMA Storage Tanks

1. **Inhibitor + dissolved oxygen integrity is finite.** A car loaded and sitting can polymerize even with MEHQ present if O₂ is consumed or temperature rises.
2. **Pressure-rated containment limits, but does not prevent, release.** Even the purpose-built 105-J tank car must vent — and the vented material is the entire content if polymerization completes.
3. **Recurrence within 2 years at the same facility** demonstrates that institutional learning from a "near-miss" does not happen automatically.
4. The Louisville plant is part of the chain of Rohm & Haas (now Dow / Trinseo etc.) MMA-handling history that goes back to the company's 1931 entry into MMA production.

## Press Coverage

Local media (Louisville Courier-Journal and local TV) covered the shelter-in-place and the venting cloud. The incident did not result in fatalities or major off-site contamination.
