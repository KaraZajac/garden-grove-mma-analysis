# NFPA 704 Hazard Diamond — Methyl Methacrylate (MMA)

**Sources:**
- NFPA 704 rating system: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFPA_704
- MMA Wikipedia entry (NFPA rating): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methyl_methacrylate

## MMA NFPA 704 Ratings

```
        Red (Flammability) = 3
        Blue (Health)       = 2
        Yellow (Reactivity) = 2
        White (Special)     = (none typically posted)
```

| Quadrant | Color | Rating | Meaning |
|----------|-------|--------|---------|
| Top      | Red — Flammability | **3** | Liquids and solids that can be ignited under most ambient conditions; flash point below 22.8 °C (73 °F). MMA flash point = 2 °C → Flammability 3 |
| Left     | Blue — Health | **2** | Intense or continued (but not chronic) exposure could cause temporary incapacitation or possible residual injury |
| Right    | Yellow — Instability / Reactivity | **2** | Undergoes violent chemical change at elevated T and P; can polymerize violently — Reactivity 2 |
| Bottom   | White — Special | (none) | No standard NFPA 704 special-hazard symbol assigned (not OX, not W, not SA) |

## Rating Scale Definitions (NFPA 704)

### Flammability (Red)

| Rating | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| 0 | Materials that will not burn under typical fire conditions |
| 1 | Substantial preheating required before ignition; flash point ≥ 93.3 °C (200 °F) |
| 2 | Moderate heat or elevated ambient T required before ignition; flash point 37.8 – 93.3 °C |
| 3 | Can ignite under most ambient conditions; flash point < 22.8 °C (73 °F) |
| 4 | Rapidly or completely vaporize at NTP and burn readily |

**MMA flash point = 2 °C → easily satisfies the "3" criterion.**

### Health (Blue)

| Rating | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| 0 | No health hazard; no precautions required |
| 1 | Irritation with only minor residual injury |
| 2 | Intense or continued (not chronic) exposure could cause temporary incapacitation or possible residual injury |
| 3 | Short exposure could cause serious temporary or moderate residual injury |
| 4 | Very short exposure could cause death or major residual injury |

**MMA = 2** — based on irritation, sensitization potential, and inhalation effects at industrial concentrations.

### Instability / Reactivity (Yellow)

| Rating | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| 0 | Normally stable, even in fire; not reactive with water |
| 1 | Normally stable, can become unstable at elevated T and P |
| 2 | Undergoes violent chemical change at elevated T and P; reacts violently with water |
| 3 | Detonation or explosive decomposition possible but needs strong initiating source |
| 4 | Readily capable of detonation or explosive decomposition at normal T and P |

**MMA = 2** — captures the autoaccelerating polymerization hazard at elevated T and P. Some references give 2 universally; certain transport / military codes use 3 because of the runaway-polymerization potential.

### Special Hazards (White)

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| OX | Oxidizer |
| W (barred) | Reacts with water dangerously |
| SA | Simple asphyxiant gas |

MMA carries none of the above by default. Some local placards use the unofficial "P" for "polymerizes" — not formally part of NFPA 704.

## Cross-Check Notes

- ChemicalBook and most generic supplier MSDSs report MMA as Health 2, Flammability 3, Reactivity 2 — matching Wikipedia.
- Some manufacturers raise Reactivity to 3 when the storage scenario is large bulk monomer with potential for runaway polymerization in the absence of cooling/agitation. That is a *site-specific risk assessment* call, not a standard substance rating.
- For the Garden Grove tank scenario, the appropriate placard reading is therefore **2 / 3 / 2** with site-specific procedures recognizing the runaway-polymerization risk that the "2" reactivity rating arguably under-represents.
