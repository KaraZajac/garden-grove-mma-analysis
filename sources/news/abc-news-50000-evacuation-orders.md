# Authorities urgently try to stop California chemical tank explosion; 50,000 under evacuation orders

**Outlet:** ABC News · **Published:** May 23, 2026, 9:01 PM · **URL:** https://abcnews.com/US/evacuation-orders-issued-california-city-chemical-tank-fails/story?id=133234846

**Authors:** Meredith Deliso, Nadine El-Bawab, and Jaclyn Lee

An overheated, pressurized tank containing methyl methacrylate at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, California, has triggered an unprecedented emergency response. Approximately 50,000 residents face mandatory evacuation orders as officials warn the situation could result in either a catastrophic explosion or a hazardous chemical spill.

**Key details:**

- **Tank status:** A 34,000-gallon industrial vessel with a malfunctioning, seized valve containing methyl methacrylate (used in aerospace manufacturing)
- **Temperature progression:** Rose from 77 degrees to 90 degrees despite overnight cooling efforts
- **Chemical quantity at risk:** Approximately 6,000–7,000 gallons
- **Incident location:** About 30 miles south of Los Angeles in Orange County

**Official statement:**
Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey characterized the situation as "the worst-case scenario I've ever faced in my career," emphasizing that authorities face two possible outcomes: the tank fails and spills chemicals, or thermal runaway causes an explosion.

**Response efforts:**
- Water cooling operations ongoing
- "Neutralizer" added to a secondary tank to prevent chain reactions
- Evacuation centers established across five municipalities
- State of emergency declared by Governor Gavin Newsom
- EPA coordinators dispatched to the facility

**Health risks:**
The chemical is primarily a respiratory irritant. Short-term exposure causes skin/eye irritation and breathing difficulties; inhalation of vapors from an explosion could trigger severe respiratory issues.
