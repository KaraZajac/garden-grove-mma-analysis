# California tank holding dangerous chemicals is set to explode or leak, officials say. Here's what to know

**Outlet:** CBS News · **Published:** Updated May 23, 2026 / 9:33 PM EDT · **URL:** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/garden-grove-methyl-methacrylate-chemical-tank-california/

**Author:** Kerry Breen, News Editor

**Location & facility:** GKN Aerospace in Garden Grove, Orange County (approximately 38 miles south of Los Angeles).

**Chemical involved:** Methyl methacrylate — approximately 7,000 gallons of a highly volatile, flammable substance used in plastic production.

**Evacuation scale:** Tens of thousands of Southern California residents evacuated; homes within one-mile radius of the facility required to leave.

**Temperature readings:** The tank reached 90 degrees Friday night, having started at 77 degrees that day, with temperatures increasing at approximately 1 degree per hour.

**Official statement:** Orange County Fire Authority Chief Craig Covey characterized this as "the most significantly dangerous event" in his decades of fire service experience, stating "This is bad as I've ever seen."

## Health & safety risks

Methyl methacrylate causes respiratory irritation, lung, skin and eye irritation, nausea, and dizziness. Since the chemical is denser than air, vapors would settle near ground level. Officials constructed sandbag barriers to prevent contamination of storm drains and waterways.

**Current status:** No injuries reported; no particles compromising public health have entered the air as of the article's publication.
