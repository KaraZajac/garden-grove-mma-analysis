# Why is Orange County chemical tank crisis so hard to fix?

**Authors:** Rong-Gong Lin II, Hannah Fry · **Outlet:** LA Times (Yahoo News syndication) · **Published:** May 23, 2026 · **URL:** https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/why-orange-county-chemical-tank-100000472.html

Substantive technical/expert analysis piece — the most useful single-source explainer on cause and outcome theories. Frames the worst-case as a **BLEVE** explicitly, and reports that a BLEVE event already occurred Thursday (an unusual claim — most other coverage describes only a leak/valve failure Thursday, not a BLEVE; treat with caution).

## Expert: Elias Picazo, USC Assistant Professor of Chemistry

Picazo is the most-cited subject-matter expert across coverage. His core mechanism:

> "The reaction is releasing heat. That's going to initiate more reaction to happen, so it might even cascade." (thermal runaway feedback)

> "Maybe the material has already polymerized in that outlet. And so you can't get anything in or out from it, because the monomer — which is a liquid — once it polymerizes, it becomes that plastic, glass-like material, and that's solid, so nothing's going to go in or out."

> "If you build too much heat or pressure, then you get the explosion. And the material is also highly flammable, in addition to being toxic, and so you can have, fumes of toxins, flames like literal fireballs, and the explosion itself."

On the cooling strategy: "definitely helping, even though the temperature inside the tank is still rising. Without it, temperatures would have risen more quickly."

On the boiling point (101 °C / ~214 °F): above 100 °C, "the pressure starts to increase dramatically, because the liquid phase becomes gas phase." This implies a hard physical threshold roughly 110 °F above where the gauge currently pegs.

## Cause theory in this piece

- **Primary:** valve failure / asset-integrity. Valves "gummed up" by self-polymerization in the outlet — blocks every solution that requires injecting or withdrawing material.
- No external sabotage or contamination angle discussed.

## Three outcome scenarios outlined

1. **Explosion (BLEVE):** "literal fireballs", debris up to half a mile, widespread damage. Worst case.
2. **Massive leak:** environmental contamination of waterways/ocean, but no further explosion risk; hazmat teams can then "neutralize and mitigate the vapors." Framed as preferable to #1.
3. **Controlled polymerization (the hoped-for outcome):** continuous water cooling slows the reaction so that liquid MMA hardens into solid PMMA inside the tank gradually, without pressure exceeding vessel limits.

## Historical analogs cited

- 2015 Exxon Mobil Torrance Refinery explosion (CSB cited outdated procedures, equipment past safe operating life — implicit parallel to GKN's prior violations).
- October 2025 Chevron El Segundo fire (blast affected homes up to one mile away).
- 1947 Texas City Disaster (ammonium nitrate, ~581 dead; cited for the regulatory-aftermath parallel re: cool storage and separation rules).

Notably **does not** mention Synthron 2006 or Rohm & Haas 2010 — the most chemically analogous incidents.

## Key uncertainties flagged by LAT

- Officials have not disclosed a specific explosion-onset temperature threshold.
- Early drone temp readings measured shell only, not bulk — caused the "stabilized" misread on Friday night.
- Exact remaining MMA inventory in the tank is not publicly known.
