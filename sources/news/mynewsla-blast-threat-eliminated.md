# Blast threat eliminated at chemical tank in Garden Grove

**Outlet:** MyNewsLA.com · **Published:** Monday, May 25, 2026 · **URL:** https://mynewsla.com/orange-county/2026/05/25/blast-threat-eliminated-at-chemical-tank-in-garden-grove/

MyNewsLA's Monday wrap-up. Adds the **storm-drain/waterway diking-and-damming** mitigation detail and confirms evacuation-zone boundary streets.

## Temperature timeline (consolidated)

- Friday morning: 77°F
- Saturday: 90°F
- Peak (over weekend): 100°F (gauge cap)
- Monday (overnight reading): **93°F**

## Tank specifications

- **Primary tank:** 7,000 gallons MMA (note: this conflicts with prior 6,000-7,000 gal liquid estimate and 34,000-gallon tank capacity)
- **Contents:** Methyl methacrylate
- Crack identified; peeling weather stripping clarified as cooling-spray erosion, not additional damage
- **No active leak** per atmospheric monitoring

## Adjacent tanks (re-stated)

- 15,000-gallon tank
- 4,500-gallon tank

## Evacuation zone boundaries

- North of **Trask Avenue**
- South of **Ball Road**
- East of **Valley View Street**
- West of **Dale Street**
- Encompasses all of Stanton and parts of Garden Grove, Anaheim, Westminster, Buena Park, and Cypress
- ~50,000 residents

## Overnight ops cadence

Firefighters conduct temperature assessments **only at night** when the tank is not exposed to sunlight — crew safety priority.

## Mitigation actions (consolidated)

- Temperature monitoring (drone + manual at night)
- **Diking and damming** to divert potential leaks away from storm drains and waterways
- Air-quality monitoring (no contaminants detected)
- Multiple evacuation centers / support facilities

## Key context

- Diking-and-damming is a more specific mitigation than prior "sandbag barriers" language and signals OCFA expects some liquid release during controlled depressurization.
- The 4,500-gallon adjacent tank figure is new specificity (prior reporting cited 15,000-gallon secondary only).
- Evacuation boundary streets (Trask/Ball/Valley View/Dale) are a clean GIS reference for the affected zone.
