# Over 40,000 evacuated in California chemical leak as Orange County officials warn tank 'is going to fail,' may explode

**Outlet:** CBS Los Angeles · **Published:** May 23, 2026 · **URL:** https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/flammable-epoxy-leak-in-orange-county-prompts-hazmat-response/

**Authors:** Matthew Rodriguez, Julie Sharp, Chelsea Hylton, Dean Fioresi, Austin Turner

Over 44,000 people were evacuated across multiple Southern California cities after officials warned of an impending failure at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove. An industrial tank containing approximately 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate — a highly flammable chemical used in plastic production — developed a compromised cooling system.

## Critical statements

Orange County Fire Authority Chief Craig Covey stated: "This thing is gonna fail. We don't know when." He added this was "as bad as I've ever seen" in his 32-year fire service career.

Covey outlined two scenarios: "One, the tank fails and spills 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot and that area. Or two, the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up."

## Incident timeline

- **Thursday, ~3:40 p.m.:** Hazmat call received; crews arrived at facility
- **Friday morning:** Officials determined the tank "unable to be secured and mitigated"
- **Saturday:** Firefighters maintained tank temperature at approximately 61 degrees using unmanned firehoses

## Evacuation zone

Homes within a one-mile radius across Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park, and Westminster were evacuated with no estimated return date.

## Health hazards

Methyl methacrylate causes respiratory irritation, potentially leading to lung, skin, and eye damage. The chemical's vapor settles due to its density exceeding air.
