# With conditions worsening, how will the Orange County chemical crisis get resolved?

**Outlet:** LA Times (DNYUZ syndication) · **Published:** May 23, 2026 · **URL:** https://dnyuz.com/2026/05/23/with-conditions-worsening-how-will-the-orange-county-chemical-crisis-get-resolved/

Companion / earlier version of the Lin-Fry resolution analysis. Slightly different framing focused on the mitigation-strategy menu and the headspace-buffer concept.

## Picazo on the boiling-point threshold

> "The reaction is releasing heat. That's going to initiate more reaction to happen, so it might even cascade."

Above 100 °C (MMA boiling point), "the pressure starts to increase dramatically, because the liquid phase becomes gas phase." This frames the practical danger threshold as 212 °F internal liquid temperature — much higher than the gauge-pegged 100 °F current reading.

## Three mitigation strategies catalogued

1. **Continuous external water cooling** — slows polymerization, lets solid form at a controlled rate (the primary active strategy).
2. **Pressurized headspace buffer** — the tank's empty headspace was designed to absorb pressure from slower curing. This is the passive mitigation. Implicitly, if curing stays slow enough, the headspace alone may be sufficient even without a working PSV.
3. **Neutralizing-agent injection into the adjacent 15,000-gal tank** — successfully done; prevents cascade ignition of the next tank. This is the only neutralizer success in coverage so far.

## The valve clog mechanism (chemistry detail)

> "A valve became clogged when MMA monomers began polymerizing into solids, blocking the primary solution pathway."

This is consistent with New Santa Ana's "MMA polymerization gel glued valve shut" framing and the OCFA "gummed up with agent" quote — i.e., self-polymerization at the outlet sealed the only intervention port.
