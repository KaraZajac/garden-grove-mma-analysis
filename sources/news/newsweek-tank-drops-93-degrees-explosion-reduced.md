# Garden Grove chemical tank drops to 93 degrees as explosion risk reduced

**Outlet:** Newsweek · **Published:** Monday, May 25, 2026, 11:23 AM EDT (updated 12:09 PM EDT) · **Byline:** Anna Skinner, Associate Editor · **URL:** https://www.newsweek.com/garden-grove-california-chemical-tank-update-crack-forecast-good-news-11990075

Newsweek's Monday update. Pairs the McGovern/Covey announcement with a weather/forecast outlook — useful for predicting whether daytime tank-heating risk persists.

## Quotes

**McGovern:**
> "We are happy to report that the threat of a BLEVE is now off the table."
> "That threat has been eliminated."

**Covey:**
> "That is incredibly positive news as we turn the corner on this incident."

## Temperature

- Tank internal temperature **fell to 93°F**

## Crack details

- Discovered during a high-risk inspection
- Acted as an unplanned pressure-release valve, allowing gases to escape
- Prevented dangerous pressure buildup
- OCFA: "one known crack on the tank"
- Atmospheric monitoring: "no chemicals leaking" — continuous live confirmation

## Evacuation

- Over 40,000 people evacuated across Garden Grove and neighboring cities
- (Newsweek uses 40,000 — diverges from CBS/ABC's "more than 50,000" figure)

## Weather forecast (new)

- Highs in **low 70s**, lows in **50s** over the next few days
- Wind gusts near Garden Grove anticipated up to **~20 mph**
- Implication: cooler weather should help suppress tank reheating during daytime operations

## Key context

- The cooler forecast is a meaningful operational tailwind for OCFA — daytime tank exposure has been the dominant risk window.
- 40k vs 50k discrepancy in evacuee count: probably reflects "primary evacuation zone" vs. "total affected including warnings/shelter-in-place."
- Confirms "no chemicals leaking" per atmospheric monitoring, even with the crack present — meaning depressurization has been gaseous, not liquid-phase release.
